## How to use this guide

This guide says how Blank Space Events sounds in writing, whoever is writing: Kiko, the team, Canvas, Cue or Claude. Read the one-line summary and the five traits first. Then check the table for your channel, and run the checklist before you send.

What this guide does not replace:

- **Message wording.** The Google Doc "BSE Message Templates Revised" is the only source of wording for templated messages (rule CUE-042, spec #391, approved by Kiko on 1 Nov 2026). This guide covers everything the templates do not: one-off emails, proposals, quotes, social posts, Messenger, website and catalogue copy.
- **Visual identity.** Logo, colour and font rules live in the BSE design system v2.0.

## The voice in one line

**A creative partner who is happy to hear from you, and exact about the details.**

The energy comes from the event and the people making it happen. The trust comes from getting dates, money and next steps right.

Signature: **Imagine. Create. Celebrate.**

## Two registers

Kiko confirmed on 7 Nov 2026 that BSE writes in two registers.

| Register | Where | How it sounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Show** | Website, social posts, catalogue headlines, package names, first-touch outreach | Playful, warm, a little bold. Short punchy lines. Wordplay on "blank" and "space" is welcome. |
| **Work** | Quotes, RFQs, invoices, payments, coordination, follow-ups | Plain, polite, exact. Facts first, one clear ask, no exclamation marks in money mail. |

One message can use both: a warm opening line in the Show register, then the details in the Work register.

## Five voice traits

### 1. Warm, never gushing

We sound glad to hear from people. We don't pile on adjectives or flattery.

- **This:** "Thanks for sending the brief."
- **Not this:** "Thank you so much for reaching out to us, we are truly so excited and honoured!"

### 2. Specific beats impressive

We name the date, the venue, the guest count and the next step. Specifics show we were listening.

- **This:** "We saw your post about your year-end party on 17 December, for 100 to 150 guests."
- **Not this:** "We'd love to help with your upcoming event!"

### 3. Energy from the event, not from punctuation

The website says "Got a space? Let's make it anything but blank." The excitement is in the idea, not in exclamation marks.

- **This:** "Almost showtime for your event. Here's what you'll need."
- **Not this:** "Almost showtime!!! So excited!!! 🎉"

### 4. One clear ask

Every work message ends with exactly what we need, from whom and by when.

- **This:** "What time would you like the performers to start and finish?"
- **Not this:** "Let us know your thoughts on everything."

### 5. Short and plain

Short sentences, everyday words, no jargon the reader has to decode. Say "event", not "project", to clients and crew.

- **This:** "If you share your theme, program and budget range, we'll send a cost estimate within two working days."
- **Not this:** "Kindly furnish the pertinent requirements so that we may facilitate the costing deliverable."

## Fixed rules (approved 29 Oct 2026)

Kiko approved these on 29 Oct 2026. They shipped in Canvas V341 and V342 and Cue 1.27.67, and automated tests check them. Do not change them without Kiko's approval.

- Open with **"Hi {first name},"**.
- Close with **"Thank you,"** and then **"Blank Space Events"** (or a team line such as Accounts Payable).
- At most **one "!"** per message, and **none** in payment or money mail.
- **No emoji** in email.
- Say **"event", not "project"**, in anything clients and crew read.
- Subjects keep the event name and codes, so Gmail search still finds them.
- In sentences, say **"Blank Space"** or **"Blank Space Events"**, not "BSE". "BSE" is fine inside the team and in SMS prefixes.

## Tone by audience and channel

| Audience or channel | Register | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website, catalogue headlines | Show | Gold standard. Bold short lines: "From first idea to final cue." |
| Facebook | Show, Taglish | Taglish is welcome here (Kiko, 7 Nov 2026). Keep it friendly and natural, not forced. |
| Instagram and LinkedIn | Show, English | Same energy as the website. LinkedIn notes stay under 300 characters. |
| Package and bundle names | Show | Playful Taglish is fine: "Pista Pinoy", "Summer Saya: Beat the heat". |
| New client outreach | Show opening, Work body | Name what we saw, what we can cover, one ask, and a time promise. |
| Client project mail | Work, warm | "Here's what's taking shape this week." Keep money and deadlines exact. |
| Suppliers (RFQ, award, payment) | Work | Itemised, dated, exact amounts. Polite and brief. |
| Crew | Work, warm | Friendly and practical. Call time and on-site contact are mandatory. |
| Payments and invoices | Work, strict | No "!", no jokes. Amount, date and reference, then thanks. |
| SMS | Work | Start with "BSE:". One fact and one link. |

English is the default everywhere except Facebook (Kiko, 7 Nov 2026: "english mostly. facebook is taglish").

## Words we use and words we avoid

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| event | project (to clients and crew) |
| bring to life, taking shape | deliverables, leverage, synergy |
| cost estimate | quotation package, proposal deck (when it's a CE) |
| Thank you, | Regards, / Best regards, / Respectfully, |
| Could you send… by {date}? | Kindly revert / Please advise ASAP |
| Blank Space, Blank Space Events | BSE (in client-facing sentences) |
| Almost showtime, What's taking shape? | Hype words such as amazing, unforgettable, brilliant, world-class (website only) |

Website lines to reuse: "From first idea to final cue." "Your space. Our magic." "We fill the blank with something brilliant." "Got a space? Let's make it anything but blank." "Clear vision. Thoughtful details. Room to surprise."

## Before and after

These examples come from real BSE messages, with names and personal details removed.

**Outreach (good, keep doing this).** "We saw your post about your year-end party on 17 December, for 100 to 150 guests. We can cover everything you listed in one team: styling, a host, photo and video coverage, and an on-the-day coordinator. If you can share your theme, program flow and budget range, we will send our rates and a cost estimate within two working days." This is specific, covers one ask and makes a time promise.

**Quick reply (good).** "Thanks for sending the brief. We can check both acoustic options for December 13, with basic sound included in each. What time would you like the performers to start and finish?" Warm, exact, one question.

**Quick reply (fix).** "Sure. Share the link and i will forward to others. Kiko" works between colleagues, but for a client: "Sure, please share the link and I'll send it to the team. Thank you, Kiko". Capital I, a greeting and the sign-off.

**Follow-up (fix).** "Was wondering if you have read my email already" reads as impatient. Better: "Following up on my note from 30 October about the payment return. Could you confirm when it will be sent?"

## Before you send: checklist

1. Is this a templated message? Use the templates doc wording.
2. Right register: Show for marketing, Work for operations and money.
3. Opens with "Hi {first name}," and closes with "Thank you,", quick replies included.
4. Dates, amounts, venue and references are exact and checked.
5. One clear ask, with who and by when.
6. At most one "!", none in money mail, no emoji in email.
7. "Event", not "project", for clients and crew.
8. English, unless it's a Facebook post.
9. Read it aloud once. If it sounds like a form letter, cut words.

## Decisions (Kiko, 7 Nov 2026)

- **Hype words** such as "unforgettable" and "brilliant" stay on the website only. Social posts, catalogues and messages don't use them.
- **Every message closes with "Thank you,"**, quick one-off replies included. "Thanks," is not used.
